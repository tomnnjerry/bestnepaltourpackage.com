Altitude sickness basics

Acute mountain sickness (AMS) can start above about 2,500 m, whatever your age or fitness.

Know the symptoms

  • Headache, with loss of appetite or nausea
  • Unusual tiredness or weakness, dizziness
  • Poor sleep

Climb slowly

  • Above 3,000 m, raise your sleeping altitude by no more than about 500 m a night
  • Take a rest day every three to four days, or for every 1,000 m gained
  • Climb high, sleep low: a short walk up in the afternoon, then sleep lower
  • Drink water, eat well and skip alcohol for the first days up high

Go down if it gets worse

  • Never go higher with symptoms; rest at the same height until they pass
  • If symptoms worsen, descend at once, even at night
  • Confusion, stumbling, breathlessness at rest or a wet cough are emergencies: descend and get medical help

General information, not medical advice. See a doctor before a high trip, especially with a heart or lung condition, and ask about acetazolamide. Check that your insurance covers helicopter rescue to the altitude you will reach.
