{% icon "route" %}Three ways in

By air, by road or by rail and road

Most travellers from the south and west of India fly. From Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and West Bengal the road is often quicker door to door.

{% icon "plane" %} By air

Tribhuvan International Airport in Kathmandu takes nearly all flights from India. Gautam Buddha International at Bhairahawa, 20 km from Lumbini, and Pokhara International have fewer international services. Airlines and schedules change, so check current status before you travel.

{% icon "car" %} By road

The main crossings for travellers are Sonauli–Belahiya (for Lumbini, Pokhara and Kathmandu), Raxaul–Birgunj (for Chitwan and Kathmandu), Rupaidiha–Nepalgunj (for Bardia and the far west), Jogbani–Biratnagar and Panitanki–Kakarbhitta (for eastern Nepal), and Banbasa–Mahendranagar. Indian cars need a customs permit at the border.

{% icon "route" %} By rail, then road

Apart from the short Jaynagar–Janakpur line, trains do not cross into Nepal. Take a train to a railhead near the border, such as Gorakhpur for Sonauli, Raxaul for Birgunj or New Jalpaiguri for Kakarbhitta, and we meet you with a car on the Nepal side.

{% icon "permit" %}Documents

What Indian citizens carry

Indian citizens do not need a visa for Nepal. Adults show a valid Indian passport or a Voter ID card at the border or the airport. Aadhaar and PAN cards are not accepted as travel documents. Children's rules depend on age. A Kailash yatra needs a passport.
