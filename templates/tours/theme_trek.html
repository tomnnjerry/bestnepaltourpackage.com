What our grades mean

We grade on the hardest days, not the average: hours on foot, height gained, the highest night and how remote the trail is.

{% icon "shield" %}Trek basics

Permits, guides and altitude

Our altitude terms{% icon "arrow" %}

{% icon "permit" %} Permits

Most treks need a national park or conservation area permit, and many need a TIMS card. Restricted areas such as Upper Mustang, Manaslu, Upper Dolpo and Kanchenjunga need a special permit, issued only through a registered agency to groups of at least two. We arrange every permit and list its cost in the quote.

{% icon "users" %} Licensed guides

Since April 2023 Nepal requires foreign trekkers in national parks and conservation areas to walk with a licensed guide. Indian citizens follow some separate rules. Every trek we sell includes a licensed guide, and porters are optional.

{% icon "altitude" %} Acclimatisation

Above 3,000 m, a common rule is to sleep no more than 300–500 m higher each night and to rest every third or fourth day. Headache, nausea and poor sleep are early signs of altitude sickness; if they get worse, go down. Our itineraries show each night's height so you can check.

{% icon "info" %}Permit fees, guide rules and restricted-area rules change. Check current status before you travel; we confirm them for your dates in writing.
