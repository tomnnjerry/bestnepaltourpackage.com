{% icon "shield" %}Before you fly

Four rules every heli tour follows

Mountain helicopters work to the weather, the thin air and your health. Knowing the rules before you book saves disappointment on the day.

Our heli and altitude terms{% icon "arrow" %}
  1. Weather decides

    Flights leave early, usually soon after sunrise, because cloud tends to build over the high valleys by late morning. The pilot can delay, divert or cancel at any point for safety. In spring and autumn most mornings fly; in the monsoon many do not. Keep a spare day in Kathmandu or Pokhara.

  2. Payload drops with height

    Thin air means less lift. A helicopter that takes five passengers out of Kathmandu may carry only two or three at a landing above 5,000 m, so groups land in two lifts or shuttle to a lower point. We ask each passenger's weight when we book, and bags are kept small.

  3. 10–15 minutes up high

    You fly from about 1,400 m to over 5,000 m in under an hour, without time to acclimatise. Landings at Kala Patthar or near Everest Base Camp are usually kept to 10–15 minutes. Longer stops, such as breakfast at Hotel Everest View (3,880 m), happen lower down.

  4. Who should not fly high

    Skip high landings if you have a heart or lung condition, uncontrolled blood pressure or a recent operation, or if you are pregnant. Babies and very young children are better kept to lower landings. Ask your doctor first; we can plan a lower route or a flight that does not land.

{% icon "info" %}Landing rules, permits and operator policies change, especially inside national parks. Check current status before you travel; we confirm the rules for your date in the written quote.
